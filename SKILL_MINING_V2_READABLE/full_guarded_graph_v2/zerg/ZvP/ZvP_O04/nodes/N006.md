# N006 — Early-Midgame Air Defense

## Node Type

DEFAULT

## Summary

Early-midgame around 360 seconds, with the opponent showing an air presence including Phoenixes and Oracles. You have a ground army with Zerglings and Queens. Maintain your ground army and consider adding anti-air units such as Hydralisks or Spore Crawlers. Continue to expand and increase production.

## When This Applies

### Opponent cues

- Enemy has a moderate air presence with Phoenixes and Oracles, supported by Stalkers. Their production is heavy and technology is heavy, indicating an air-oriented strategy.
- Treat remembered or observed Enemy Intelligence as partial and uncertain.

### Own cues

- Your economy is heavy and you are expanding. You have a ground army with Zerglings and Queens, moderate production, and moderate technology. Defense is heavy.
- Use the live observation to check army supply, free supply, resource bank, technology, and current queues.
- These cues are approximate and do not all need to be true.

## Recommended Strategic Direction

Maintain your ground army and consider adding anti-air units such as Hydralisks or Spore Crawlers. Continue to expand and increase production.

Phoenixes and Oracles can harass and deal damage. By adding anti-air, you can defend against these threats while maintaining your economy.

## Conditional Safety Guardrail

Before adding anti-air units, verify that your ground army is sufficient to defend against potential ground attacks. If your ground defense is weak, prioritize ground units before investing in anti-air.

**Recheck when:**  
Recheck if the opponent adds more air units or if you need to adjust your composition.

## What This Does NOT Mean

This node is not an instruction to reproduce a historical action sequence.

Choose exact macro actions from the current live observation, not merely because a unit or structure appeared in historical evidence.

## Transition Goal

Transition to a composition that can handle both air and ground threats, such as adding Hydralisks or Mutalisks.
