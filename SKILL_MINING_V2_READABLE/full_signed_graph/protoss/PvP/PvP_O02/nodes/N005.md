# N005 — Early Game Stabilize and Develop

## Node Type

DEFAULT

## Summary

In the early game, you have a heavy defense posture and moderate economy, while the opponent has a heavy economy and moderate production. The opponent's army is unknown, but you have a defensive advantage. The goal is to stabilize and then develop. Increase defense and economy, continue production, and increase technology. Strengthen your ground army to prepare for potential aggression.

## When This Applies

### Opponent cues

- Enemy Intelligence is consistent with an unknown posture. No reliable combat-unit cues have been observed. Production appears moderate, technology investment appears heavy, and exact hidden counts remain unknown.
- Treat remembered or observed Enemy Intelligence as partial and uncertain.

### Own cues

- Your own posture is broadly unknown-oriented, with light or uncertain production and heavy technology investment. You have a heavy defense posture, which suggests you have invested in defensive structures or units. Your economy is moderate, and you have not expanded aggressively.
- Use the live observation to check army supply, free supply, resource bank, technology, and current queues.
- These cues are approximate and do not all need to be true.

## Recommended Strategic Direction

Increase defense and economy, continue production, and increase technology. Strengthen your ground army to prepare for potential aggression.

With a heavy defense posture, you are well-positioned to withstand early pressure. Increasing your economy and technology will give you a long-term advantage. Strengthening your ground army ensures you can defend against any early attacks.

## What This Does NOT Mean

This node is not an instruction to reproduce a historical action sequence.

Choose exact macro actions from the current live observation, not merely because a unit or structure appeared in historical evidence.

## Transition Goal

Transition to a more aggressive posture once you have a solid economy and technology base. Aim to establish map control and pressure the opponent.
