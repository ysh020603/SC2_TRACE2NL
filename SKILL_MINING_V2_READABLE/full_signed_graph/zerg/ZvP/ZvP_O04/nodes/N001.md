# N001 — Early Game Economy Focus

## Node Type

DEFAULT

## Summary

At the start of the game, focus on expanding and building a strong economy. The opponent's tech is unknown, so maintain flexibility. Continue expanding and building economy. Maintain production of workers and start producing basic units like Zerglings and Queens for defense. Keep scouting to detect any early pressure.

## When This Applies

### Opponent cues

- The opponent is also in the early game with an unknown army composition. They have a heavy economy and expansion, moderate production, and heavy technology investment. No combat units have been reliably observed.
- Treat remembered or observed Enemy Intelligence as partial and uncertain.

### Own cues

- You are in the early game with a ground-oriented macro posture, heavy economy, and heavy expansion. Production is moderate, and technology is light or uncertain. You have no significant army yet, but you are developing.
- Use the live observation to check army supply, free supply, resource bank, technology, and current queues.
- These cues are approximate and do not all need to be true.

## Recommended Strategic Direction

Continue expanding and building economy. Maintain production of workers and start producing basic units like Zerglings and Queens for defense. Keep scouting to detect any early pressure.

A strong economy is the foundation for all later transitions. By focusing on economy now, you can outproduce the opponent in the midgame. The opponent's heavy tech investment suggests they may be going for a tech-heavy composition, so you need to be ready to adapt.

## What This Does NOT Mean

This node is not an instruction to reproduce a historical action sequence.

Choose exact macro actions from the current live observation, not merely because a unit or structure appeared in historical evidence.

## Transition Goal

Transition to a more solid ground army with Zerglings and Queens, while continuing to expand. Aim to have a strong economy by the time the opponent's tech comes online.
