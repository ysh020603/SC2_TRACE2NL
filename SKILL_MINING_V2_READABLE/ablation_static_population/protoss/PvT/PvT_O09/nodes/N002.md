# N002 — Early-Midgame Ground Macro with Heavy Tech

## Node Type

COMMON

## Summary

In early-midgame phase with a ground-oriented army, heavy production, heavy technology, and heavy economy. Expansion posture is light or uncertain, defense is light or uncertain, and air presence is light or uncertain. Continue to strengthen your ground army and increase production. Maintain your economy and tech development. Consider securing a third expansion soon to support your heavy economy.

## When This Applies

### Opponent cues

- Opponent is Terran. Their early game strategy is unknown; they could be going for a quick expand, a timing attack, or tech into cloaked units. Keep scouting to detect threats.
- Treat remembered or observed Enemy Intelligence as partial and uncertain.

### Own cues

- You have a developing ground army (Zealots, Stalkers) and are investing heavily in tech and economy. Production is heavy, but your expansion is not yet secure. Defense is light, so you need to be careful about early pressure.
- Use the live observation to check army supply, free supply, resource bank, technology, and current queues.
- These cues are approximate and do not all need to be true.

## Recommended Strategic Direction

Continue to strengthen your ground army and increase production. Maintain your economy and tech development. Consider securing a third expansion soon to support your heavy economy.

Your heavy tech and economy investment will pay off in the midgame, but you need to survive the early-midgame transition. A solid ground army with Stalkers can defend against most early Terran aggression.

## What This Does NOT Mean

This node is not an instruction to reproduce a historical action sequence.

Choose exact macro actions from the current live observation, not merely because a unit or structure appeared in historical evidence.

## Transition Goal

Transition to a more solid midgame position by securing your expansion, getting Observer for detection, and preparing for potential tech choices (e.g., Colossus or air).
